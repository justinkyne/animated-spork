# “Paying It Back, Dad”

A satirical, vertical (9:16) political ad. In May 2026 a Putin ally paid for Don Jr.'s
wedding parties. Asked whether the money was repaid, President Trump said his son told him,
*“No, I'm paying it back, Dad.”* The ad has ordinary Americans try the same excuse on
their own bills. It doesn't go well.

**Logline:** *A Putin ally picked up their tab. Who's picking up yours?*

## Why it should travel

1. **Hook in under 2 seconds.** It opens like a dreamy wedding video, with a sweet
   narrator and a music box, but the post header reads *“Sponsored by **a Putin ally**.”*
   The narrator gets as far as *“Paid for by…”* before a record scratch cuts her off.
2. **Receipts, literally.** The corruption appears as an itemized bill:
   $100,000/night island, ~$70,000 fireworks, sailor-suit dancers, paid via a Dubai entity.
   The stamp is in Russian. Every number on it is sourced.
3. **The affordability turn uses their own words.** The President's “I'm paying it back,
   Dad” is the whole joke. Four different everyday voices try that line on their bills
   (groceries, rent, car insurance, the IRS) and get stamped DECLINED, EVICTION NOTICE,
   POLICY CANCELED, and AUDITED. This is the privilege gap, shown and not lectured.
4. **Scale that anyone can feel.** One night on that island costs more than the median
   American household earns in a year. The fireworks alone cost more than two average
   American weddings.
5. **A built-in meme format.** The line *“I'm paying it back, Dad”* is designed for
   stitches and duets: people film themselves saying it to their own bills.
   Hashtag: **#PayingItBackDad**.
6. **The close asks a question instead of making a claim.** *“In America, that's called a
   gift. In Moscow, it's called an investment.”* Then: *“A Putin ally picked up their tab.
   Who's picking up yours?”* A question invites comments, and comments feed the algorithm.

**Sound.** A narrator carries the story. The music bed changes with each act: a wedding
music box, then a tense ticking pulse for the investigation, then a sarcastic oom-pah
under the “let's all try it” montage, then a low drone and heartbeat for the close. The
music ducks under the voice. Effects land the jokes: record scratch, receipt printer,
rubber stamps, a card-declined buzzer, and a deflating “wah” into *“Weird.”*
Every line is also on screen, so the ad still works with the sound off.

The narrator and the four everyday voices are stock synthetic voices (Kokoro TTS).
They are not clones of anyone. The President's and the spokesperson's words are read
as quotes, not performed as impersonations.

### Cutdowns

- **Short (36s):** the sponsored hook, then the bill and stamp, then the “Dad” quote and
  stamp, then “Let's all try it” with groceries and rent, then “Only works when Dad's the
  President,” then the close and the end card.
- **Teaser (17s):** opens cold on the President's quote (the on-screen label and the
  narrator supply the context), then groceries and rent, then “Only works when Dad's the
  President,” then the end card.

## Before you post

- [ ] **Re-check the repayment story.** As of Sept. 30, 2026, no proof of repayment had
  been provided. If proof surfaces, change the “NONE PROVIDED” stamp (in `index.html`,
  search for `s5-stamp`) and re-render.
- [ ] **Re-read the ProPublica piece and the Time/Axios profiles** to confirm the
  receipt's line items and the “May 19–20, Putin's delegation to China” wording.
- [ ] **Organic vs. paid.** Posting organically needs no disclaimer. If a political
  committee pays to place the ad, add the FEC “paid for by” notice:
  `node render.mjs --paidfor "Committee Name"`. That puts it on the end card and keeps it
  on screen for 4s. Platform rules differ: Meta and Google require political-advertiser
  verification, and TikTok does not accept paid political ads at all. This is not legal
  advice; check with counsel or the platform's policy page.
- [ ] Keep the “Satire” line in the caption as well as on the end card.
- [ ] The voiceover is synthetic. It isn't an impersonation, but some platforms ask you to
  tick an “AI-generated content” box for synthetic audio. Check the upload screen.

## Rendering

```sh
# preview: open index.html in any browser (tap to play; choose Full / Short / Teaser)
python3 -m http.server 8000   # then visit http://localhost:8000/paying-it-back-dad/

# re-render videos (needs Playwright + an ffmpeg with libx264)
node paying-it-back-dad/render.mjs                  # full cut
node paying-it-back-dad/render.mjs --cut short
node paying-it-back-dad/render.mjs --cut teaser
node paying-it-back-dad/render.mjs --still 23.9 --out poster.png

# regenerate the voiceover after editing a line (needs kokoro-onnx; see the script header)
python3 paying-it-back-dad/voiceover/make_vo.py --models <kokoro model dir>
```

Every frame and sound is a pure function of time, so renders are frame-exact and
repeatable. Scene lengths live in the `LEN` table in `index.html`, and every cue is an
offset from the start of its scene. If you change a voice line, check that it still fits
its scene (`make_vo.py` prints each clip's length). The renderer normalizes loudness to
−16 LUFS, which suits social platforms.
